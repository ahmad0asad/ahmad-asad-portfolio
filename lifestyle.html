Multitemporal Sentinel-2 Framework for Urban Soil Sealing Detection

Primary Doctoral Research · University of Bari Aldo Moro & PNRR Fellowship
Elsevier RSASE 2026 (Vol. 43, 102231)

The Scientific & Practical Problem: Monitoring soil sealing (impervious surface expansion) across heterogeneous peri-urban and rural landscapes has historically been hindered by severe spectral confusion. On a single-date optical satellite image, dry bare agricultural soil, harvested crop fields, and unpaved roads exhibit spectral reflectance curves almost identical to asphalt, concrete, and roofing materials. This leads to substantial overestimation and false positives.

The Algorithmic Solution: To resolve this spectral ambiguity, I designed an automated multitemporal framework that exploits temporal phenology. Rather than relying on static images, the algorithm constructs seasonal multi-band composite stacks from Sentinel-2 MSI data. Because agricultural fields undergo dramatic vegetation growth and post-harvest plowing cycles throughout the year, their spectral variance over time is high. In contrast, artificially sealed surfaces maintain stable, invariant reflectance across all seasons.

1

Multispectral Band Harmonization

Integrated Sentinel-2 10m visible and NIR bands (B2, B3, B4, B8) with 20m red-edge and SWIR bands (B5, B6, B7, B8A, B11, B12), resampling and normalizing surface reflectances.

2

Temporal Feature Engineering & Index Formulation

Extracted multi-seasonal statistical percentiles (10th, 50th, 90th) and temporal variance across key spectral indices, including NDVI, NDBI (Normalized Difference Built-Up Index), and MNDWI.

3

Machine Learning Classification Pipeline

Trained and optimized Random Forest ensemble models using spatial cross-validation to eliminate spatial autocorrelation. Validated across contrasting Mediterranean and temperate agro-climatic zones, achieving high detection accuracy without site-specific hyperparameter tweaking.

Sensors & Resolution Sentinel-2 MSI (10m / 20m)
Machine Learning Multitemporal Random Forest
Key Libraries Python, Scikit-learn, Rasterio, GeoPandas
Outcome Published in Elsevier RSASE

Pure-Pixel Spectral Analysis & Sub-Pixel Spectral Unmixing

Visiting Research Scholar Appointment · University of Almería, Spain
Research Mobility (Jan 2026 – Jul 2026)

The Challenge of Mixed Pixels: At spatial resolutions of 10m to 30m, satellite pixels are rarely spectrally homogeneous. A single pixel along an urban fringe often encapsulates a blend of asphalt, rooftop tile, grass, and tree canopy. Traditional "hard" classification algorithms force a binary label on the whole pixel, hiding early-stage land sealing and failing to quantify low-density artificial surfaces.

Implementation & Radiative Transfer Modeling: During my research mobility at the University of Almería, I focused on endmember extraction and spectral unmixing workflows:

1

Automated Endmember Extraction

Identified the purest spectral signatures (endmembers) directly from satellite scenes for high-albedo impervious surfaces, low-albedo impervious surfaces, green vegetation, and bare soil.

2

Fully Constrained Linear Spectral Unmixing (FCLSU)

Developed least-squares unmixing algorithms enforcing non-negativity and sum-to-one constraints, decomposing mixed pixel reflectance into fractional abundance maps (0% to 100% sealed material per pixel).

3

Validation Against Ground & Orthophoto Truth

Benchmarked sub-pixel estimations against ultra-high-resolution aerial imagery and field radiometric data in southeastern Spain and southern Italy, achieving reliable quantification of diffuse sealing.

Methodology Fully Constrained Linear Unmixing
Spectral Domain VIS - NIR - SWIR (0.4 – 2.2 µm)
Key Tools Python, NumPy, SciPy, Spectral Python
Target Terrains Mediterranean Arid & Semi-Arid

Cloud-Native Geospatial Automation with Google Earth Engine & openEO

Scalable Big Data Infrastructure for Multi-Temporal Surface Monitoring
Cloud Architecture

Overcoming Local Storage Bottlenecks: Processing multi-year, continental-scale satellite archives involves tens of terabytes of optical data. Downloading these volumes to local workstations is inefficient and limits reproducibility. I structured fully cloud-native workflows using Google Earth Engine (Python and JavaScript APIs) and openEO.

Cloud Workflow Highlights:

  • Automated Cloud & Shadow Masking: Integrated the `s2cloudless` machine learning probability masks and scene classification layers (SCL) to remove cloud and shadow pixels dynamically without manual intervention.
  • Server-Side Batch Execution: Constructed server-side reducer functions to compute annual medians, standard deviations, and seasonal indices directly on Google’s distributed cluster.
  • openEO Standardized Pipelines: Implemented openEO process graphs to ensure algorithm workflows are vendor-neutral and portable across different Copernicus cloud data providers.
  • Direct Spatial Export: Automated tiling and export of Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs (COGs) into spatial storage buckets for downstream analysis.
Platforms Google Earth Engine & openEO
Languages Python, JavaScript
Input Archives Sentinel-2 L2A & Landsat 8/9
Deliverable Automated Batch Ingestion Pipelines

Mapping the Photovoltaic Landscape of Puglia Using Open Energy Data

Spatial Database Modeling & Energy Transition Analytics
Springer LNCS 2027 (Vol. 16762)

Territorial Context: In the Puglia region of southern Italy, utility-scale photovoltaic (PV) power plants have expanded rapidly, often converting high-value agricultural soils into solar energy farms. Regional authorities and researchers needed an empirical spatial database to quantify this land transition and monitor spatial trends.

Spatial Database Engineering: I assembled and harmonized open territorial data, cadastral parcels, and official renewable energy registries (GSE) across the Puglia region:

1

PostGIS Spatial Database Administration

Designed spatial schemas in PostgreSQL with PostGIS, executing spatial indexing (GIST), geometric validity repair, and spatial joins across hundreds of thousands of cadastral parcel polygons.

2

Land Transition & Displacement Analysis

Intersected photovoltaic plant footprints with historical Land-Use/Land-Cover (LULC) inventories to calculate the exact hectares of olive groves, arable land, and natural vegetation transformed into energy infrastructure.

3

Evidence-Based Territorial Reporting

Delivered spatial transition matrices and cartographic models to guide sustainable territorial planning policies in southern Italy.

Spatial Database PostgreSQL / PostGIS
Data Sources GSE Open Energy Data, Cadastral Maps
Techniques Topological Modeling & Spatial Joins
Publication Springer LNCS ICCSA Workshops

SoilEye: An Interactive Web App Prototype for Soil Sealing Monitoring

Full-Stack WebGIS Tool for Environmental Decision-Makers
Springer LNCS 2026 (Vol. 15891)

Bridging Science and Public Policy: Satellite research findings often remain locked within academic journals or complex desktop GIS software that municipal technicians and environmental planners cannot easily operate. I developed SoilEye, a lightweight interactive WebGIS application that makes soil sealing monitoring instantly accessible.

System Architecture: SoilEye connects a responsive client-side interface with cloud-served geospatial raster tiles. Users can pan and zoom across regional monitoring areas, drag an interactive temporal slider to compare historical baseline imagery against present-day status, draw custom polygons of interest, and instantly generate summarized zonal change statistics without touching command-line scripts.

Application Type Interactive WebGIS Prototype
Front-End Stack HTML5, Modern CSS, Vanilla JavaScript
Mapping Services Leaflet, WMS/WMTS Tile Services
Publication Springer LNCS ICCSA Workshops
Part 2 · Personal Discipline

Lifestyle, Fitness & Nutrition for Cognitive Focus

How I manage physical conditioning, daily routine, and balanced nutrition to stay sharp and energized for demanding computational research.

The Connection Between Physical Fitness and Mental Clarity

Conducting satellite algorithm research, debugging complex geospatial scripts, and working through mathematical models requires hours of uninterrupted mental concentration in front of computer displays. Rather than treating fitness and nutrition as an afterthought, I view physical conditioning as the essential foundation that keeps my energy steady, clears mental fatigue, and maintains sharp cognitive thinking throughout the week.

Nutrition for Cognitive Clarity

What I eat directly impacts how I think. I consciously structure my diet around clean, nutrient-dense Mediterranean whole foods available here in Puglia, Italy.

  • Healthy Fats: High-quality local extra virgin olive oil as the primary cooking and dressing fat.
  • Quality Protein: Fresh Adriatic fish, pasture-raised eggs, and traditional legumes (chickpeas, fava beans, lentils).
  • Micronutrients: Generous servings of seasonal vegetables, dark greens, and fruit for natural antioxidants.
  • Zero Brain Fog: Avoiding ultra-processed foods, refined sugar spikes, and heavy midday meals prevents energy crashes and maintains steady afternoon concentration.
  • Hydration: Drinking 3+ liters of water daily to prevent dehydration-induced headaches and cognitive sluggishness.
Balanced Whole Foods Steady Glucose Hydration

Cardiovascular Endurance

Aerobic conditioning builds stamina. Spending hours analyzing spatial matrices can be mentally exhausting; regular cardiovascular training keeps my heart and lungs strong.

  • Coastal Running: Regular easy-paced running along the seaside promenade of Bari to decompress and enjoy natural light.
  • Cycling: Moderate-intensity weekend cycling exploring the roads and countryside of Puglia.
  • Consistent Aerobic Base: Maintaining an easy conversational pace that builds cardiovascular resilience without creating excessive physical exhaustion.
  • Mental Reset: Using aerobic sessions as dedicated screen-free time where research ideas often naturally fall into place.
Running in Bari Coastal Cycling Aerobic Stamina

Strength & Posture Care

Counteracting the desk-bound posture. Data science and geospatial programming inevitably involve extended seated periods, which can lead to back and shoulder strain.

  • Compound Movements: 3 to 4 resistance sessions per week focusing on squats, deadlifts, pull-ups, and core stabilization.
  • Spinal Health: Reinforcing posterior chain muscles (upper back, glutes, hamstrings) to prevent rounded shoulders and neck tension.
  • Daily Mobility Drills: 10 to 15 minutes of hip flexor stretching, thoracic rotations, and shoulder mobility every morning.
  • Workstation Posture: Alternating sitting and standing at the desk to keep circulation active throughout the day.
